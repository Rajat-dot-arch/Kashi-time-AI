Location: Dashashwamedh Ghat

History:
Dashashwamedh Ghat is one of the oldest and most important ghats on the Ganga in Varanasi. Its name means “the ghat of ten horse sacrifices.” According to tradition, Brahma performed ten Ashwamedha sacrifices here to welcome Shiva. The present structure was rebuilt by Peshwa Balaji Baji Rao in 1748 and later improved by Ahilyabai Holkar in 1774.

Significance:
It is the site of the famous Ganga Aarti, held every evening. It is also a major ritual bathing and pilgrimage spot, located close to Kashi Vishwanath Temple. The ghat is central to Varanasi’s religious and cultural life.

Changes Over Time:
- 1748: Rebuilt by Peshwa Balaji Baji Rao.
- 1774: Improved by Ahilyabai Holkar.
- Late 20th century: Evening Ganga Aarti became a major public ritual.
- 2021: Kashi Vishwanath Corridor improved access to nearby areas.

Key Events:
- 1748: Peshwa reconstruction.
- 1774: Ahilyabai Holkar renovation.
- Present: Daily Ganga Aarti.
