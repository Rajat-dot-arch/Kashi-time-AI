Location: Kashi Vishwanath Temple

History:
The Kashi Vishwanath Temple is a major Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Shiva in Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh. Its history is marked by repeated destruction and reconstruction. It was destroyed by Qutb-ud-din Aibak in 1194. A later temple was rebuilt in the 16th century by Raja Man Singh. Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb destroyed it in 1669 and built the Gyanvapi Mosque on the site. The current temple was constructed by Ahilyabai Holkar in 1780.

Significance:
It is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and among the holiest Shiva temples in India. The main deity is a Shiva lingam known as Vishwanath or Vishweshwara, meaning “Lord of the Universe.” It is the spiritual center of Kashi and draws millions of pilgrims.

Changes Over Time:
- 1194: Destroyed by Qutb-ud-din Aibak.
- 1585: Rebuilt by Raja Man Singh.
- 1669: Destroyed by Aurangzeb; Gyanvapi Mosque built.
- 1780: Current temple built by Ahilyabai Holkar.
- 1835: Maharaja Ranjit Singh donated gold for the dome.
- 2021: Kashi Vishwanath Corridor inaugurated.

Key Events:
- 1194: Major destruction.
- 1669: Destruction by Aurangzeb.
- 1780: Reconstruction by Ahilyabai Holkar.
- 2021: Corridor inauguration by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
